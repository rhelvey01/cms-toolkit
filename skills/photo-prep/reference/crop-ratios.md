# Target Crop Ratios (SafeSpace check)

These come from Cendyn's guidelines for video placement, which describe the actual site zones
images and video share. Until a specific site's design spec says otherwise, treat these as the
ratios a photo may need to survive being cropped to. If an image's placement is unknown, check it
against all of them rather than assuming.

| Placement | Ratio guidance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Masthead / hero banner | Very wide, varies (e.g. ~1920×500, ~3.8:1) | Highest-risk crop — needs the most peripheral room around the subject. Keep shots simple and airy; avoid subjects that need to stay large in frame. |
| Mid-page push | 4:3, 3:4, or 1:1 (varies) | More forgiving than masthead, but still requires margin — this ratio range spans both landscape and portrait crops of the same source image. |
| Carousel / gallery | 16:9 or 4:3 (varies) | Follows masthead/push framing conventions. |
| Footer | Follows masthead guidance | Same very-wide constraint as the masthead. |
| Full-length / body videos | No fixed ratio | Not a SafeSpace concern — these aren't recropped the way banner/gallery placements are. |

## How to apply this in the SafeSpace check

A photo that's a **hero candidate** or destined for **General Hotel Shots / Activities /
Lifestyle** categories (the categories most likely to run in a masthead or gallery) should be
checked against the full ratio range above. A photo destined for a narrower use — e.g. a single
dining detail shot only ever used inline — may only need the mid-page push check. When in doubt,
check against all of them; over-checking costs nothing, under-checking produces a bad crop later.

## Underlying principle (from the source guidelines)

> "The best way to ensure your focal area is not cropped out of a responsive website is to shoot
> with the subject matter centered in the frame... Leave plenty of room around your subject
> matter so the web designer can crop images appropriately for the space. Often the same image
> needs to run in a variety of height/width ratios — it isn't always proportional."
